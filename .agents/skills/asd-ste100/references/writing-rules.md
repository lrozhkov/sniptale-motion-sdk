# Writing Rules and Sources

ASD-STE100 defines a controlled form of English for technical documentation. This reference summarizes rule categories. It does not reproduce the standard or its approved-word dictionary. Obtain the authoritative document through the [official download request](https://www.asd-ste100.org/STE_downloads.html).

The source skill identifies Issue 9, dated January 2025. It describes 53 writing rules across nine sections. The dictionary restricts approved words by meaning and part of speech. A separate technical vocabulary can define necessary domain terms.

## Structure

Use active voice when the actor is known and relevant. State one instruction per sentence. Keep procedures within 20 words per sentence and descriptions within 25. Keep each paragraph on one topic with at most six sentences. Use lists for sequences and parallel conditions.

Use infinitives, imperatives, and simple present, past, or future where they preserve meaning. Use past participles as adjectives where appropriate. Avoid unnecessary compound tenses. Preserve a compound form when it carries uncertainty or current relevance that a simple tense would remove.

Remove semicolons. Avoid noun clusters longer than three words. Keep subjects, verbs, and articles explicit. Do not compress text into ambiguous fragments.

## Vocabulary

Use one term for one action. Prefer direct verbs over nominalized actions. Avoid ambiguous phrasal verbs and unsupported marketing adjectives. Keep necessary technical terms consistent.

Approved-word compliance requires the official dictionary. This repository does not reproduce it. Structural checks and plain wording alone do not establish dictionary compliance.

## Semantic checks

Preserve modality, requirements, exceptions, numbers, and scope. A hedge expresses confidence. Removing it can change the claim. Do not add causal explanations or stronger guarantees during rewriting.

Distinguish domain actions before treating them as synonyms. Schema validation, runtime proof, a compatibility check, and user confirmation can be separate operations. A heuristic synonym finding requires contextual inspection.
